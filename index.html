A Tarsia puzzle maker that runs in the browser

Type your question and answer pairs, choose a shape, and print a cut-out puzzle. It is free, there is no account, and nothing is installed — the puzzle above is being drawn by your own browser as you work.

A Tarsia is a set of pieces that fit together into a larger shape, where two pieces may only touch where a question meets its answer. Students cut the pieces apart and rebuild the shape, so the finished shape is itself the mark scheme: if it comes out right, the answers were right. It is the kind of task that gets a quiet class talking about maths, which is most of why teachers keep using it.

What you can make

The shapes are generated rather than copied from a fixed table, so they come in sizes the original desktop app never offered.

Any tiled shape can have its outside edges labelled with plausible wrong answers. Left blank, those edges quietly tell a student which pieces face outwards, and a class that spots this can assemble a good deal of the puzzle without answering much.

Changing the shape or the size never throws away what you have already typed, so the options are safe to try out. When you are finished, Download PDF gives you the pieces to cut up and the answer sheet to check them against.

It also opens files from the desktop app

If you have a folder of .fjsw files made in Formulator Tarsia — your own, or a department's collection built up over years — press Open… and pick one. It is read, drawn, and editable: change a question, watch the puzzle redraw, print the result. You do not need the desktop app installed, or Windows to run it on.

How to open an .fjsw file covers which templates are supported and what happens to the ones that are not.

Your puzzles stay on your computer

There is no server here. The page is a few static files; reading the file you open, laying out the puzzle and building the PDF all happen inside your browser, and nothing you open or type is sent anywhere. Load the page once and you can carry on with the network off.

This is worth being plain about, because the alternative is normal. Most "convert your file online" tools work by uploading your file to somebody else's machine, and a school's assessment material is not a holiday photo.
